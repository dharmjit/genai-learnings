Watch the 'comm ms' column: nearly constant, because the
all-reduce moves the WEIGHTS, not the data. As the batch grows that

The measured bubble should track (P-1)/(m+P-1) = 1/(m+1) closely
at small m and then flatten -- past a point the microbatches get so
small that the GEMMs stop filling 188 SMs and per-launch overhead
dominates. That tension, bubble vs. kernel efficiency, is exactly
what real schedulers (GPipe, 1F1B, interleaved) are tuning.

PP (split layers, m=8)         19.731     1.63x          128          256   yes

How to read this:
  DP is fastest and needs no comm -- but every GPU stores the whole
     model, so it does nothing for a model that does not fit.
  TP halves the weight memory but pays an all-reduce per block, and
     that volume grows with the batch. Over PCIe it is the loser;
     over NVLink (~10x this link) it would be competitive.
  PP also halves weight memory and moves the LEAST data by far,
     which is why it is the strategy of choice across slow links --
     at the cost of pipeline bubbles and scheduling complexity.

Real systems compose all three: TP inside a node, PP across
nodes, DP over the whole lot.
